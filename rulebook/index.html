Overview

You are not a player — you are the owner and general manager of a professional soccer club. Every decision a real front office makes — who to draft, how to line up, what to charge for tickets, which sponsor to sign, which trades to make, and how far you push for a championship — is yours to make, in writing, every round. Your grade is not just whether you win. It is whether you can run a sports business and explain why you made the calls you made.

1. The Season Scorecard — Your Grade

100 points across four components. Ranking and Revenue are scored relative to the rest of the league — 1st place earns full points, and every place below the one above it costs a flat 1 point, not a fraction of the whole component spread evenly down to zero. Finishing last still costs you real points relative to your classmates, but it does not wipe out the entire component the way an even 1st-to-0 spread would.

ComponentPointsQuestion it answersHow it's scored
Standings Rank40Did your team win?1st = 40, −1 pt per place down (last of 18 = 23)
Playoff Qualification5Did you make the playoffs?Flat: full 5 pts for finishing in the top 6, 0 otherwise — not tiered, since finishing 1st vs. 6th is already rewarded by Ranking
Business Revenue35Did you run it as a business?1st = 35, −1 pt per place down (last of 18 = 18) — ticket + sponsorship + national TV + local TV revenue
Decision Rationale20Could you explain your calls?Average grade on the written explanation you submit with your Weekly Lineup, as a % of 20

A well-reasoned decision that loses to bad luck scores exactly as well, on Rationale, as one that wins. A last-place team still earns 23 of 40 Ranking points and 18 of 35 Revenue points just for being in the league, and a last-place team that runs a genuinely profitable franchise can still score well on Revenue. There is more than one way to earn a strong grade here.

Worked Example

A team that wins the league (40/40 — full ranking points), qualifies for the playoffs by finishing top 6 (5/5), finishes 3rd in league revenue out of 18 teams (33 pts), and averages a strong rationale score (17.5 of 20) lands at 40 + 5 + 33 + 17.5 = 95.5 out of 100.

4. Player Ratings

Every player carries six ratings, 0–99 (the same convention as most sports video games):

RatingWhat it measures
ATTAttack — finishing and creativity, the biggest input to scoring chances
DEFDefense — tackling, positioning, shot-stopping
PACPace — speed and recovery, a secondary contributor both ways
PHYPhysical — strength and stamina, a secondary contributor both ways
OVROverall — a quick-reference blend of the four above (NOT what the match engine uses directly)
Star PowerMarketability — deliberately independent of skill. Drives sponsorship eligibility and ticket demand.

Weekly Player Condition

ATT and DEF above are a player's fixed BASE ratings — what actually goes into a match is scaled by that player's CONDITION for that specific round, redrawn every round for every player. A player who was excellent last week can be off this week, and vice versa — last week's lineup is not automatically this week's right call.

ConditionMultiplierOdds
Poor0.85x10%
Below Average0.93x20%
Average1.00x40%
Good1.07x20%
Excellent1.15x10%

Condition is drawn once per player per round, from a fixed formula (your season seed + the round number + that player's ID) — not random in a way that can be gamed, and not knowable before the league office publishes it. It is published before that round's Weekly Lineup deadline, both on the live dashboard (each roster card shows a Condition badge per player) and as a standalone report (engine/generate_weekly_conditions.py) specifically so it's real information your starting XI and formation choice should account for, not a surprise revealed only in the result. A bench player in Excellent form can outperform a starter having a Poor week — reading the weekly report before you submit is part of the job.

Injuries

Every player who actually STARTS a match carries a 6% chance of picking up an injury that round — unlike the routine week-to-week Condition roll above, an injury is a CONSEQUENCE of playing, not a fresh dice roll every week, and it doesn't clear on its own until it runs its course.

WhatDetail
Chance per start6%
Duration1–3 rounds, randomly, once injured
Effective multiplier while injured0.55x

While injured, a player's Condition badge shows "Injured" (not one of the five routine tiers above) with how many rounds they're still out for — visible on the dashboard and in the weekly report exactly like ordinary form, before you submit. An already-injured player who gets started anyway just keeps counting down; they cannot get newly hurt on top of an existing injury. Bench them, or play them at a real, known cost — that decision, and being able to see it coming, is the point.

5. The Draft

Before the season begins, every owner drafts a full roster from the shared player pool. Two hard rules apply:

  • Roster size is fixed at 18 players, filling every slot your formation choices require.
  • Total roster salary may never exceed the $18,000,000 salary cap — at draft time or afterward.

Undrafted players remain Free Agents, signable later in the season.

How the Draft Actually Runs

A real live snake draft — 18 rounds × 18 teams — is hundreds of sequential picks, far more than one class period holds. So Draft Day is a reveal, not a wait:

  1. Before Draft Day: submit a ranked Draft Board of at least 25 players (more than the 18 you need, so you still have real choices left once some of your targets are gone).
  2. Draft order: one randomized snake order (Round 1 picks 1…18, Round 2 picks 18…1, alternating), drawn reproducibly from the season seed — the exact order can be independently re-verified after the fact, same principle as every match result.
  3. Live in class: the league office runs the resolution engine once, in front of everyone. At your turn each round, you get the highest-ranked player still on YOUR board who's still available and still fits under your remaining cap space — instant, no live back-and-forth required.
  4. If your whole board runs out before your roster is full (bad luck — too many targets taken ahead of you): autopick takes the highest-OVR player still available and affordable — same "league office auto-fills" fallback used for a missed Weekly Lineup submission or a missed Trade Day pairing.

6. Weekly Operations

Every round, before the deadline, each owner submits a Weekly Lineup & Strategy form: formation, strategy, starting XI by name, a ticket price if home that round, and a 2–4 sentence rationale (graded — see the grading table below).

Check that round's Player Condition report first (see Player Ratings above) — it's published before the deadline specifically so your starting XI and formation choice can react to who's actually in form this week, not just who has the highest base rating.

If you do not submit: the league office auto-fills your highest-rated available player at each position, Balanced strategy, Standard pricing. Your match is still played and still counts — you simply forfeit rationale credit, since there is no decision to grade.

8. How Match Results Are Determined

Results are not a coin flip, and not roster OVR alone — every result traces to the lineup, formation, and strategy you actually submitted:

  1. Each starter's ATT/DEF is first scaled by their Weekly Player Condition for this round (see Player Ratings above), then your starting XI's (condition-adjusted) ATT/DEF become one Team Attack and one Team Defense score, weighted by formation.
  2. Your Strategy choice shifts that balance further.
  3. The home team gets a fixed +5% Attack bonus.
  4. Attack vs. opposing Defense produces an expected-goals (xG) number for each side — a big mismatch caps out rather than producing an unrealistic blowout.
  5. The actual score is drawn randomly around each side's xG — the deliberate "luck" every real match has. A stronger team is more likely to win, never guaranteed to.

Every match's random draw is seeded from the season's fixed seed plus that exact matchup — any result can be independently re-verified after the fact, and nothing is adjustable after the fact by the league office.

9. Ticket Sales & Attendance

As the home team, you set a ticket price tier. Attendance responds to price AND to your recent form and starting-XI Star Power:

TierPriceEffect
Budget$15Fills more seats, caps per-ticket revenue
Standard$30Neutral default
Premium$50Highest per-ticket revenue, but demand drops sharply without form/Star Power to justify it

There is no universally correct price — a struggling, low-Star-Power team pricing Premium will earn LESS than pricing Standard or Budget. Read your own team's market every home match.

10. Sponsorship Deals & Negotiation

Sponsors pay you — there is no cost to sign anything, matching how real sponsorship deals work. Brands are grouped into 6 categories (real clubs carry exactly one jersey sponsor, one kit manufacturer, one official airline — not an unlimited pile of deals), and every team MUST sign exactly one brand per category before the draft ends — this is mandatory, not optional. Multiple teams may hold the same brand (Nike sponsors many real clubs at once); the "slots" number on each brand is a league-wide cap, not an exclusivity lock.

On your turn (same turn-order format as the player draft, cycling through all 6 categories), target an open brand in whichever category you're filling. Two separate things are on the table:

Revenue

Take the listed Base revenue exactly (guaranteed, zero risk), or Negotiate. Negotiating lands you anywhere from −10% to +10% of the brand's base revenue, set by your Negotiating Leverage (built from your roster's average Star Power, plus a standings bonus once the season starts): leverage 0 lands at −10%, leverage 50 lands exactly at base, leverage 100 lands at +10%. Negotiating is not risk-free the way taking Base is — a weak hand can genuinely land below base.

The commission: whenever a negotiated deal lands away from base — in EITHER direction — that gap is ALSO credited as bonus revenue to whichever team's student played the sponsor rep in that negotiation. Land above base (good for the signing team) and the overage goes to the rep's team; talk the team down below base (good for the sponsor) and the savings goes to the rep's team instead. Landing exactly at base earns nobody a commission, since nothing was actually negotiated. Never a deduction from the signing team's own booked revenue either way.

Clause

A separate ask: push for a looser clause at Modest / Bold / Very Bold intensity, gated by the same leverage number. Ask beyond what your leverage supports and the brand walks away on the clause specifically — that costs you nothing on the revenue side, and you can simply not push the clause further.

What the Clause Actually Means

The performance clause is not flavor text -- it is real revenue at risk, and each clause level sets its OWN rank threshold to keep the money. Checked ONCE, at the end of the season (Round 17, the final round): if your team's FINAL rank misses that specific deal's threshold, that deal claws back a flat 20% of its revenue -- the clause you negotiated sets how hard the bar is to clear, not how much you lose if you miss it:

ClauseRequired final rankPenalty if missed
StrictTop 520%
StandardTop 920%
LooseTop 1420%
NoneNo requirement0%, ever

This is checked PER DEAL, off your one final rank -- a team can clear a loose-clause deal's easy bar while missing a strict-clause deal's demanding one in the exact same season, whether those deals are both sponsors or one is a sponsor and the other your Local TV Deal (see the TV / Broadcast Revenue section below -- Local TV deals carry a clause too, checked against this exact same table). A strict clause pays more up front for a real bet that you'll actually contend; a loose clause is the safer, lower-upside insurance policy. Checked exactly once, at season end -- never applied twice to the same deal.

Full live marketplace (open slots, who's signed what) and a negotiation simulator to rehearse your ask before your draft turn: see the separate Sponsorship Marketplace site.

11. TV / Broadcast Revenue

League-Wide (National) TV Deal

Base: $800,000 per team.

Standings bonus: Top team at split time: +1,000,000. 2nd: +600,000. 3rd: +300,000. Bottom 3 teams: +0 (no penalty beyond missing the bonus).

Star Power bonus: If a team's average starting-XI Star Power ranks in the league's top 3 at split time, add +500,000 (broadcasters pay for star wattage independent of the standings) -- this can stack with the standings bonus.

Split timing: After round 3 of the 17-round regular season (18 teams, round-robin) -- adjust proportionally if team count changes.

Local TV Deals

A per-team regional broadcast contract, separate from and additional to the league-wide national TV deal above -- modeled on how real clubs also carry an individual local/regional media deal that varies hugely by market size, on top of a shared national split. Assigned once, right after Draft Day (engine/generate_local_tv_deals.py -> data/local_tv_deals.json), and fixed for the whole season like a real regional broadcast contract -- not renegotiated mid-season.

Market tiers: Every team is randomly assigned exactly one of three market tiers, split as evenly as possible across the league and drawn reproducibly from season_seed: Major Market ($1,200,000 base, strict clause), Mid Market ($900,000 base, standard clause), Small Market ($700,000 base, loose clause) -- higher-value markets carry a stricter clause, the same pattern Sponsorship's brand table uses.

Star Power modifier: If a team's average starting-XI Star Power ranks in the league's top 3 at split time, its Local TV Deal pays +15% that round -- same top-3 threshold as the national deal's star bonus, scaled off the team's own base (proportional) rather than a flat dollar add, since a Major Market base and a Small Market base already differ so much in size.

Payout timing: Paid out at the same midseason split point as the national TV deal (after Round 3 of the 17-round regular season) -- one TV Revenue Day covers both national and local money at once.

Local TV revenue flows into the team's budget like any other revenue; it never raises the salary cap itself.

Local TV Rights Negotiation

Right after Draft Day, every team negotiates its Local TV Deal live, in class, against a rotating peer playing the network rep (engine/generate_tv_negotiation_rotation.py pairs everyone once, no repeat partners; an odd team count leaves one bye, filled by the instructor). Exactly like Sponsorship, TWO separate things are on the table -- same mechanic, same functions in engine/negotiation.py. Revenue: take the market-tier Base rate exactly (guaranteed, zero risk), or Negotiate to land anywhere from -10% to +10% of it, set by your Negotiating Leverage (built from your roster average Star Power, plus a standings bonus once the season starts). Clause: a separate ask -- push for a looser clause than your market tier's base clause, at Modest/Bold/Very Bold intensity, gated by the same leverage number against a required threshold; an unsuccessful ask can walk away (clause stays at base, no revenue impact). The commission: whenever a negotiated rate lands away from base -- in EITHER direction -- that gap is ALSO credited immediately (not at the Round 3 split) as bonus revenue to whichever team played the network rep. Above base rewards the negotiating team's win; below base (a good deal for the network) rewards the rep instead, same size commission, mirrored direction -- same either-direction logic as a sponsorship commission. Landing exactly at base earns nobody a commission. This negotiation sets the RATE and the CLAUSE only; the money itself is still paid at the Round 3 split above, with the Star Power modifier still applied on top then, and the clause is checked once at season end exactly like a sponsorship clause (see clause_enforcement above -- the same rank thresholds apply to Local TV deals). The League-Wide (National) TV Deal is not part of this exercise -- it is a shared, formula-only split with no natural second party to negotiate against.

Clause

Exactly like Sponsorship, a Local TV Deal's clause is a separate ask from its rate: push for a looser clause than your market tier's base clause, at Modest / Bold / Very Bold intensity, gated by your same Negotiating Leverage number. Ask beyond what your leverage supports and the deal walks away on the clause specifically — that costs you nothing on the revenue side, and you can simply not push the clause further. See "What the Clause Actually Means" in the Sponsorship Deals & Negotiation section above for the rank-threshold table — the same thresholds and the same 20% penalty apply to a Local TV Deal's clause, checked once at season end off your one final rank.

12. Trades & Free Agency

Deadline: Trades may be proposed through Round 6 of the 17-round regular season. No trades are permitted in Rounds 7-17 or at any point after -- this prevents last-minute roster stacking right before the playoff-qualification picture is set, the same reason real leagues carry a trade deadline.

Salary cap rule: A trade is only valid if BOTH teams remain at or under salary_cap (see league_config.json) after the trade completes.

Cash considerations: Teams may include cash (from their budget, not a salary-cap item) as part of a trade package.

Approval: All trades are submitted via the Trade Proposal form and require instructor (league office) approval before taking effect -- this is the checkpoint for grading trade rationale and for vetoing collusive or lopsided trades.

Mandatory Mid-Season Trade Day

One mandatory in-class Trade Day, held mid-season (right after the Round 3 TV-revenue split, well ahead of the Round 6 deadline). Every team is paired with exactly one partner from a published rotation (engine/generate_trade_day_rotation.py, same no-repeat pairing method as the Sponsorship Negotiation rotation) and must negotiate and submit ONE completed Trade Proposal by end of class -- real assets, or a good-faith minor swap if the two sides can't agree on more, since grading is on the negotiation and rationale, not on landing a 'good' trade. An odd team count leaves one team unpaired; the instructor fills in as that team's trade partner, the same convention already used for the Sponsorship Negotiation rotation's bye. This is a floor, not a cap: the ordinary voluntary trade window stays open before and after Trade Day, through Round 6.

13. Standings

Win = 3 points, draw = 1 point, loss = 0 points. Ties broken first by goal difference, then total goals scored.

14. Playoff Qualification

No bracket games are played. The top finishers in the final regular-season standings (see the qualification line marked on the dashboard, and the bonus table below) are recognized as Playoff Qualifiers -- a season-ending designation, not a separate elimination tournament.

StageBonus
Finish in the top 6 of the final standings$400,000

Paid once, at season's end, to every team that finishes inside the qualification line -- not stacked or tiered by seed. 1st place and 6th place both earn this bonus equally; regular-season standings already reward finishing 1st over 6th through the Ranking component of the Season Scorecard.
